\begin{definition}[Thermodynamics]
    Thermodynamics, derived from the ancient Greek words \textit{therme} (heat) and \textit{dynamis} (power), is the branch of physics that deals with the relationships between heat, work, temperature, and energy.
\end{definition}
\begin{remark}
    Thermodynamics does not give microscopic details of the system\footnote{Classical thermodynamics cannot describe microscopic systems directly, but modern extensions like stochastic thermodynamics and statistical mechanics can.}, but rather describes the macroscopic properties and behaviors of systems in terms of a few state variables.
\end{remark}
