\begin{definition}[Orbital Angular Momentum]
    The orbital angular momentum $\vec{L}$ of a particle is defined as $\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}$, where $\vec{r}$ is the position vector and $\vec{p} = m\cdot\vec{v}$ is the linear momentum, both in the x-y plane.
\end{definition}
$L_{z} = rmv$ is the component of angular momentum along the z-axis (circular trajectory). For a solid object $L = I_{0}\omega$ where $I_{0}$ is the moment of inertia and $\omega$ is the angular velocity.

Periodic circular motion $I = \frac{q}{T}$, where $T$ is the period.

A charged point particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ moving in a circular path with period $T$ will produce a circulating current $I = \frac{q}{T}$ and this current will produce a magnetic moment $\mu = \frac{IA}{c} = \frac{q}{2mc}mvr = \frac{q}{2mc}L_{z}$.
